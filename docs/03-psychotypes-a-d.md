Psychotypes A–D

Functional Psychology — Public Structural Description

Author: Vladimir Gulyaev

This document provides a public high-level description of Psychotypes A, B, C and D within Functional Psychology.

The purpose is to describe the internal functional criterion of each psychotype rather than reduce diagnosis to visible traits, stereotypes or behavioral checklists.

This document does not disclose proprietary diagnostic algorithms, scoring logic, interview sequences or private verification procedures.

⸻

1. General Principle

In Functional Psychology, a psychotype is not defined by a simple list of observable characteristics.

The same visible behavior may arise from different underlying functional architectures.

For example:

* confidence does not automatically indicate A
* social sensitivity does not automatically indicate B
* precision does not automatically indicate C
* practical behavior does not automatically indicate D

The central diagnostic question is:

What internal functional criterion organizes the person’s perception, decisions and behavior?

Therefore, Functional Psychology distinguishes between:

visible behavior
and
underlying functional architecture

The underlying architecture is primary.

⸻

2. Two Functional Vectors

The four psychotypes belong to two complementary vectors.

1 → A / B
0 → C / D

Vector 1

Associated with:

* expansion
* search
* creation
* development
* movement toward new possibilities

Vector 0

Associated with:

* preservation
* mastering
* stabilization
* implementation
* maintenance of existing structures

The vectors are not hierarchical.

Neither is considered superior.

⸻

Psychotype A

3. Functional Position of A

Psychotype A represents the male functional form of Vector 1.

At the architectural level, A is oriented toward:

* expansion
* formation of new contours
* creation of new systems
* movement beyond existing boundaries
* initiating new structures
* changing the environment rather than merely adapting to it

The central functional tendency is not simply leadership.

It is the tendency to create or expand a contour.

⸻

4. Internal Functional Criterion of A

The internal criterion of A can be described as orientation toward:

What can be created?
What can be expanded?
What can be changed?
What new system can be formed?

A is therefore especially sensitive to:

* unrealized possibilities
* limitations of existing structures
* opportunities for expansion
* situations requiring a new system rather than maintenance of the old one

This does not mean that every A constantly creates businesses, leads organizations or behaves dominantly.

Context, development, resources and life circumstances matter.

⸻

5. A and Surface Behavior

An A may temporarily perform highly routine or technical work.

This alone does not make the person C or D.

For example, under survival conditions, A may operate inside an existing system because circumstances require it.

The functional question remains:

What happens when sufficient resources, autonomy and opportunity become available?

A tends toward formation or expansion of a contour rather than indefinite adaptation to an externally defined one.

⸻

6. Mature and Immature Expression of A

A mature form may involve:

* strategic expansion
* creation of viable systems
* responsibility for consequences
* ability to build structures that outlast the founder
* integration of other functional types

An immature or disintegrated expression may involve:

* expansion without sufficient structure
* impulsive destruction of existing systems
* inability to maintain what has been created
* confusion between dominance and actual system creation

These are states or developmental differences, not definitions of the psychotype itself.

⸻

Psychotype B

7. Functional Position of B

Psychotype B represents the female functional form of Vector 1.

At the architectural level, B is associated with:

* development
* recognition of emerging possibilities
* expansion through relational systems
* adaptation to new opportunities
* integration of new possibilities into human environments
* movement toward what can develop further

The defining criterion is not sociability, emotionality or desire for approval.

⸻

8. Internal Functional Criterion of B

B is especially oriented toward:

What can develop here?
What new possibility is emerging?
What is becoming meaningful?
What can grow through interaction?

An important feature of B is that an external signal may initiate an internal process of recognition.

This should not be interpreted simplistically as:

B needs approval

A more accurate public formulation is:

external signal → internal recognition → development of meaning

The external environment may activate something that B then recognizes as personally significant.

⸻

9. B and Verification

One of the important distinctions in Functional Psychology is that B-verification is not identical to dependence on external evaluation.

The relevant question is not:

Does the person want others to approve?

The relevant question is:

How does information from the environment participate in the person’s internal recognition and development of meaning?

This distinction is essential because many people of different psychotypes seek approval, reassurance or feedback for entirely different reasons.

⸻

10. B and Surface Similarity

B can be confused with socially developed C.

A high-status or highly competent C may:

* communicate confidently
* manage people
* appear socially sophisticated
* demonstrate strong interpersonal skills

This does not automatically make C into B.

The distinction must be based on the criterion of decision and functional orientation, not presentation style.

⸻

11. Mature and Immature Expression of B

A mature B may:

* recognize emerging opportunities
* facilitate development in people or systems
* create relational expansion
* connect new possibilities with existing human structures
* translate potential into development

An immature or disintegrated form may show:

* unstable dependence on signals from the environment
* difficulty distinguishing meaningful recognition from emotional reinforcement
* excessive adaptation
* loss of internal coherence under stress

Again, these manifestations should not be confused with the definition of the psychotype itself.

⸻

Psychotype C

12. Functional Position of C

Psychotype C represents the female functional form of Vector 0.

At the architectural level, C is associated with:

* preservation
* mastering
* stabilization
* reliable implementation
* reproduction of established systems
* maintenance of quality
* filling an existing contour with functioning content

C is not defined simply by conservatism, caution or precision.

⸻

13. Internal Functional Criterion of C

C is oriented toward questions such as:

How does this system work?
How can it be mastered?
How can it be reproduced reliably?
How can quality be maintained?
How can an existing structure be filled and stabilized?

The functional emphasis is on mastering and reliable reproduction of a valid structure.

⸻

14. C and Competence

C can become exceptionally competent.

When a process is:

* understood
* structured
* learnable
* repeatable
* quality-controlled

C may perform it at a very high level.

This can produce:

* technical mastery
* operational reliability
* excellent professional execution
* consistent quality

The absence of spontaneous system creation does not imply low intelligence or low competence.

⸻

15. C and Combinatorial Thinking

In Functional Psychology, C may demonstrate strong combinatorial ability.

This means that C can:

* study an existing process
* understand its components
* reproduce it accurately
* combine known elements effectively
* optimize within an established framework

This differs from creating a fundamentally new contour.

The distinction is functional, not evaluative.

⸻

16. High-Rank C

A socially developed, high-status or professionally successful C may resemble B externally.

Such a person may:

* lead teams
* speak confidently
* display refined social skills
* manage relationships effectively
* operate in complex environments

The distinction must therefore be based on:

decision criterion
not
social appearance

The question is whether the person primarily develops new possibilities or masters and stabilizes a valid existing structure.

⸻

17. Mature and Immature Expression of C

A mature C may provide:

* stability
* quality
* continuity
* reliability
* expert mastery
* strong implementation

An immature or disintegrated expression may involve:

* excessive dependence on established procedures
* difficulty functioning when no valid model exists
* rigid reproduction
* overreliance on known structures

These are developmental manifestations rather than the essence of C.

⸻

Psychotype D

18. Functional Position of D

Psychotype D represents the male functional form of Vector 0.

At the architectural level, D is associated with:

* maintenance
* stabilization
* practical functioning
* reliable execution
* operating inside an existing contour
* sustaining established systems

D is not synonymous with degradation.

This distinction is fundamental.

⸻

19. Internal Functional Criterion of D

D is oriented toward questions such as:

What needs to be done?
How can this be maintained?
How can this system continue functioning?
What practical action is required inside the existing contour?

The emphasis is often on direct functional execution within a defined structure.

⸻

20. D and Practicality

D may be especially effective in environments where:

* tasks are concrete
* the structure is established
* practical execution matters
* immediate functional outcomes are visible
* continuity is more important than redesign

This may make D highly effective in many technical, operational and practical roles.

⸻

21. The D Paradox

A useful public distinction concerns the relationship between local criticality and long causal chains.

D may demonstrate:

* strong criticism of immediate details
* practical detection of local problems
* direct response to visible failures

while simultaneously having greater difficulty with:

* long abstract causal sequences
* distant systemic consequences
* highly extended chains of inference

This should not be interpreted as a universal cognitive deficit.

It is a functional hypothesis used within the system and requires contextual verification.

⸻

22. D and Significant Others

In some contexts, D may be strongly influenced by the risk of disappointing or failing a significant person or reference figure.

This can affect:

* decision-making
* compliance
* conflict behavior
* anxiety
* practical choices

This is not a standalone diagnostic criterion.

It must be interpreted together with the broader functional architecture.

⸻

23. D and Organizational Degradation

Functional Psychology distinguishes:

Psychotype D
from
systemic degradation under D dominance

The claim is not:

D = degradation

Rather, a working organizational hypothesis proposes that when maintenance becomes the dominant system-forming function in a context that still requires development, criteria may gradually narrow.

Possible consequences can include:

* substitution of development with maintenance
* reduction of complexity
* lowering of criteria
* preference for immediate functionality over long-term development

This concerns organizational dynamics, not personal worth.

⸻

24. Mature and Immature Expression of D

A mature D may provide:

* reliability
* practical stability
* continuity
* execution
* maintenance of essential systems
* operational resilience

An immature or disintegrated expression may show:

* excessive narrowing to immediate tasks
* resistance to long-term change
* dependency on existing authority or structure
* inability to recognize when maintenance itself has become insufficient

Again, state and psychotype must remain separate.

⸻

Comparative Architecture

25. A–D at a Glance

A highly simplified public comparison is:

Psychotype	Vector	Functional orientation
A	1	Create and expand new contours
B	1	Develop emerging possibilities
C	0	Master, reproduce and stabilize existing structures
D	0	Maintain and practically sustain existing structures

This table is intentionally simplified.

It should not be used as a standalone diagnostic test.

⸻

26. What This Model Does Not Mean

The model does not mean:

A = leader
B = emotional
C = perfectionist
D = worker

Such descriptions are too superficial.

Similarly:

1 ≠ good
0 ≠ bad
A ≠ superior
D ≠ inferior

Functional Psychology is based on differences in architecture, not rankings of human value.

⸻

27. Same Behavior, Different Architecture

Two people may perform the same behavior for different functional reasons.

For example, two people may both:

* manage a company
* speak publicly
* work in medicine
* run a team
* build a business
* teach
* perform technical work

Yet their underlying criteria may differ substantially.

Therefore:

profession ≠ psychotype
role ≠ psychotype
status ≠ psychotype
skill ≠ psychotype

⸻

28. Context Matters

Functional Diagnostics evaluates behavior across multiple contexts.

A single situation is insufficient.

Publicly, the principle can be represented as:

stable architecture
must explain
multiple contexts over time

This helps distinguish:

* underlying type
* adaptation
* role behavior
* stress response
* learned skill
* compensation
* temporary state

⸻

29. Development Does Not Convert A into C or B into D

A person can acquire functions associated with other types.

For example:

* A can learn maintenance
* B can develop technical discipline
* C can become an effective leader
* D can acquire sophisticated communication skills

This does not necessarily change the original psychotype.

Functional development expands the repertoire available to the person.

⸻

30. Interaction Is Central

Psychotypes should not be interpreted only as isolated categories.

Functional Psychology places major emphasis on:

what happens between architectures

Different combinations may produce:

* attraction
* misunderstanding
* development
* conflict
* complementarity
* compensation
* dependency
* role redistribution

This interaction layer is addressed separately in the public documentation.

⸻

31. E Is Separate

A–D are underlying functional architectures.

E is different.

A / B / C / D = psychotype
E = disintegration state

A person in E still has an underlying psychotype.

Therefore, disorganized, unstable or dysfunctional behavior must not automatically be treated as a new personality type.

⸻

32. Diagnostic Boundary

This document provides public conceptual orientation only.

Reliable diagnosis cannot be reduced to:

* a four-question quiz
* visible traits
* occupational stereotypes
* one life event
* one relationship
* one conversation
* self-identification with a description

The private diagnostic system includes additional verification procedures that are not published in this repository.

⸻

33. Public Summary

At the highest level:

A
creates and expands
B
recognizes and develops emerging possibilities
C
masters and stabilizes established structures
D
maintains and practically sustains established structures

Within the binary architecture:

1 → A / B
Expansion and Development
0 → C / D
Stabilization and Maintenance

And:

E → Disintegration State
